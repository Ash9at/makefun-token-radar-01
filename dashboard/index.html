The model implied value = TVL × peer multiple × size × growth × seasoning × breadth Peer multiple: the median market-cap/TVL of the same category's live tokens in this snapshot, shrunk toward the global median by sample count — thin categories inherit the global answer rather than posing as precise. Only tokens the market actually prices count as peers: 15 snapshot rows record a token at a $0 market cap, and a dead token is not evidence. Size: the market pays fewer dollars of cap per dollar of TVL the bigger a protocol already is (fitted slope −0.48 on log–log), so large rows are scaled down, capped at ×0.33–×3.00. The cap binds on the largest protocols, deliberately: uncapped, the backtest showed the fitted slope over-discounting them. Growth damps the 7-day TVL change, falls weighted twice rises; seasoning rewards years run with no token (an unreported age earns a neutral ×1.00, never an invented one); breadth adds a little per extra chain. No name is special-cased. How to read this: rank 1 is the largest estimated token value if the protocol launched today and the market priced its TVL the way it prices live peers'. Model accuracy — the model, graded on protocols it priced blind

A leave-one-out backtest inside this snapshot: each of the 128 protocols whose token already trades was removed from the peer set, priced by the model exactly as a pre-token protocol is priced, and compared with the market cap it actually carries. Inside every trial the peer set, its medians and the size slope are refitted from scratch without the subject; only the model's constants are fixed across trials. Fifteen further rows record a token at a $0 market cap — no price to predict against — and are excluded as subjects. The bands on the board come from these trials: each row carries the interquartile range of the model's own errors for its pricing set — the set's own range when it has at least 8 blind trials, the global range otherwise (the "own-band pricing only" filter above shows just the rows carrying their own). Band coverage is measured leave-self-out: each trial's band is rebuilt without that trial before checking whether its actual cap fell inside.

This model vs the simpler approaches — same 128 blind trials

Typical miss is 10 raised to the median absolute log10 error; bias is the mean log10 error, negative meaning the model leans low. The two "removed" rows are ablations on identical trials: they price what each part of the model earns. A split-half check — each half of the subjects selecting the model's constants for itself, then scoring the other half — measured the selection cost of the fixed constants at approximately zero.

Where the blind predictions landed

Per category — weakest first, nothing hidden

Bridges and DEX rows carry the widest bands on the board because the model prices them worst: their market caps are set by things TVL does not record. Band cover is published per category because the average flatters some rows — an interquartile band should contain about half its trials, and where it plainly does not (DEXs, whose errors are fat-tailed), the table says so. One limit is structural: the trials price protocols that did launch, so they cannot validate the seasoning adjustment — a subject's recorded age includes its post-token years. Seasoning rests on the length of the record, not on a measured effect, and the ablation in the build notes shows it is accuracy-neutral. The backtest runs on the same snapshot the model is fitted to — leave-one-out removes each subject from its own peer set, but the model's constants were chosen with these trials in view. Read this as calibration, not out-of-sample proof.
